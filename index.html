School Safety Drill for Active Attack Incidents

Practical school planning framework with editable templates for the contingency plan, PTA/PNP coordination, drill execution script, and after-action report.

Current reference point: Public DepEd records show a Nationwide Simultaneous School Safety Drill for Active Attack Incidents scheduled for September 3, 2026. Public reporting also emphasizes trauma-informed protocols and prohibition of hyper-realistic threat simulations.

1. Legal / Policy Basis

The drill is anchored in DepEd disaster-risk-reduction and school-safety policy and the applicable 2026 memoranda. The school's activity should follow its own approved Contingency Plan for Active Attack Incidents and any controlling regional/division memorandum.

Always verify the latest signed memorandum applicable to your school, division, and region before implementation.

2. Core Objectives

  • Practice protective actions and safe decision-making.
  • Test communication, accountability, and coordination.
  • Identify gaps in locks, routes, alarms, reunification, and documentation.
  • Keep the exercise age-appropriate and trauma-informed.

Three-Phase Drill Model

1

Lockdown / Lockout

Secure the classroom/area, follow the approved school signal, silence devices, remain calm and await authorized instructions.

2

Evacuation

When a safe route is confirmed by authorized personnel, teachers guide learners along the approved route and maintain accountability.

3

Reunification / Compliance

Proceed to the designated reunification or assembly area, conduct headcounts, and practice cooperation with responders.

Pre-Drill Planning Checklist

A. Build / Update the Plan

Review the school's active-attack contingency plan; identify decision authority, communications, room-security measures, evacuation routes, reunification, accountability, responder coordination, and recovery support.

B. Activate the DRRM Team

Assign planning, communications, safety, documentation, first aid/support, accountability, and coordination roles consistent with the school's organizational structure.

C. Outside Evaluator

Invite at least one appropriate external observer/evaluator from a response or safety agency, such as LGU, PNP, BFP, LDRRMO, or another authorized emergency-response partner.

D. Facilities & Equipment

Check locks/security measures, posted information, safe areas, routes, emergency communications, accountability tools, first-aid arrangements, and family reunification location.

E. Consent & Assent

Provide advance information to learners, parents, and personnel and implement the required written parental consent and learner assent process specified by the controlling policy.

F. Learner Orientation

Explain the drill before the activity. Learners should know the approved signal, protective actions, evacuation process, accountability, and reunification procedures.

Drill-Day Execution

1

Lockdown / Lockout

  • Activate the approved drill signal.
  • Teachers direct learners to follow the school-approved protective action.
  • Secure doors/windows as specified by the contingency plan.
  • Silence devices and maintain calm.
  • Do not instruct learners to confront or stop an attacker.
2

Evacuation

  • Evacuate only when a safe route is confirmed by authorized personnel.
  • Follow the pre-established route and alternate route where applicable.
  • Keep accountability throughout movement.
  • Follow responder directions.
3

Reunification / Compliance

  • Proceed to the designated area.
  • Conduct headcounts and report missing/unaccounted persons through the command structure.
  • Practice family-reunification procedures and cooperation with responders.
  • End the drill only through an authorized all-clear.

Hard Limits — Do Not Cross

The exercise must not become a realistic attack simulation. The objective is protective-action practice, not fear generation.

No active-aggressor role-play.
Do not use people acting as attackers.
No realistic weapons.
Do not use firearms, bladed weapons, blunt weapons, or realistic replicas.
No simulated gunfire or injury.
Avoid realistic sound effects, fake blood, or graphic injury scenes.
No deception or surprise threat.
Participants should know it is a drill.
No confrontation training for children.
Learners are not instructed to attack, chase, or stop an aggressor.
Trauma-informed approach.
Use age-appropriate language and provide support pathways.

Post-Drill Actions

Debrief

Meet with the School DRRM Team and outside evaluator. Capture strengths, gaps, timing, communication failures, security issues, accountability problems, and needed improvements.

Psychological Support

Provide access to appropriate guidance or psychological first-aid/support resources and allow learners/personnel to raise concerns.

After-Action Report

Document observations, findings, corrective actions, responsible persons, and target dates; submit through the applicable school/division reporting process.

Update the Plan

Revise the contingency plan, checklists, maps, contact lists, and training based on lessons learned.
