01 — Policy basis

Why this drill happens

The drill runs under DepEd's Disaster Risk Reduction and Management (DRRM) guidelines and 2026 memoranda directing all public and private schools to conduct a School Safety Drill for Active Attack Incidents, built on each school's own contingency plan.

  • Every school develops or updates a School Contingency Plan for Active Attack Incidents, furnished to the LGU, PNP, and PTA.
  • An outside observer/evaluator — from the LGU, PNP, BFP, or Local DRRM Office — attends every scheduled drill.
  • This guide adds the operating detail for two specific field procedures — lockdown and lockout — and what happens after a real incident, not just a drill.

02 — Preparation

Before the drill

  • Activate the School DRRM Team; build or review the Contingency Plan; invite an external observer.
  • Secure classroom locks/bars, signage, safe rooms, evacuation routes, and a designated family reunification area.
  • Secure written parental consent and student assent before any student takes part.
  • Orient students beforehand — this drill is never a surprise. Walk through the alarm signal, and where/how to secure their specific room.

03 — Field procedure

LOCKDOWN

When the threat is inside

Called when there is an active security threat, dangerous intruder, violence, or other immediate danger inside the premises.

1
Activate the lockdown alarm/notification. Push button → bells, sirens, or an open/banging ring — same idea as a bank's alarm button.
2
Bring people inside from hallways or outdoor areas, when it is safe to do so. Move toward a secured room / nearest secured area.
3
Lock and secure doors. Multiple or double locks, door bars — whatever the room has.
4
Move away from doors and windows and stay out of sight. No peeping. Don't do anything that could attract attention.
5
Remain quiet and silence phones when appropriate. Relax, don't panic, be calm — panic itself can attract a threat. Slow exhale, then inhale.
6
Do not open the door for anyone unless the authorized authority confirms it is safe. "Authorized" means PNP, or a trusted barangay official/teacher you recognize.
7
Do not leave the secured area until an official all-clear is given. Wait for the signal — it should come from a peace officer or barangay official, not a rumor.
8
Contact emergency authorities when safe and provide necessary information. Where you are, what you saw or heard, how many people are with you.
9
After the threat is resolved, conduct accountability, first aid, and incident documentation. Leave it to the police — they prepare the official incident report.
Calling the police

When — the moment you recognize the threat. Don't wait for the school alarm or a fully secured room first; if you can do it safely without giving away your position, call now.

Who — whoever first sees or hears the threat calls immediately with what they know. The Communications Lead then becomes the ongoing point of contact with PNP/BFP and meets responding officers at the gate.

What to say — exact location (building, floor, room), what you actually saw or heard, how many people are with you, and whether the threat is ongoing or has moved. Stay on the line if asked.

04 — Field procedure

LOCKOUT

When the threat is outside

Used when there's a security or safety threat outside the facility — a dangerous person nearby, or an external security incident.

1
Announce the lockout through the established communication system. Use a pre-arranged signal, distinct from the lockdown signal.
2
Secure all exterior doors and gates.
3
Bring people inside if it is safe to do so. Even if a student isn't yours — bring them in for safety, then account for them.
4
Restrict entry and exit until the situation is resolved. Let no one in or out.
5
Continue normal indoor activities if they can be conducted safely. Less alarm keeps everyone further from danger — once it's clear, go back to normal.
6
Monitor the situation and maintain communication with authorities. By observing and listening — not by stepping out to check.
7
Do not allow unauthorized persons to enter. Anyone trying to get in under threat could be a suspect or conspirator — treat unexpected entry with caution.
8
Conduct accountability and safety checks as appropriate. Issue an official all-clear before normal access is restored.
Calling the police

When — as soon as the outside threat is recognized, before or alongside announcing the lockout.

Who — whoever first spots the danger calls it in. The Communications Lead maintains contact with PNP/BFP for updates and coordinates who's cleared to come in or leave once it's safe.

What to say — what you're seeing and where, direction of movement if any, and a request for patrol presence near the gate if needed.

Lockdown — threat is inside, secure people inside rooms.
Lockout — threat is outside, secure the facility from the outside threat.

05 — Execution

Drill-day phases

A scheduled drill runs in three phases, whichever mode (lockdown or lockout) it's built around:

  • Lockdown/lockout — the mode-specific steps above, timed and observed.
  • Evacuation — once a safe path is confirmed, move to the assembly area with hands visible.
  • Reunification/compliance — headcount at the assembly area, then controlled release at the family reunification area.

For the full minute-by-minute run sheet, announcer script, and observer checklist, use the companion Drill-Day Script and Run Sheet document.

06 — Non-negotiable

Hard limits for any drill

Never in a drill

  • No simulated gunfire, real/replica weapons, or live role-players portraying an attacker.
  • No deception or surprise sprung on learners without prior orientation.
  • No simulated injuries or graphic/hyper-realistic elements.
  • Learners are never instructed or expected to confront or physically stop an attacker.

07 — Real incident

Post-incident recovery

This section applies after an actual incident — not a drill A shooting incident is a police matter first. The steps below describe the school's role alongside law enforcement, quarter-applied through drills so the routine is already familiar if it's ever needed for real.

Safety & security restoration

Coordinate with the PNP and other authorities to determine when the school can safely reopen or resume activities.

This comes after investigators have assessed the scene. Formal turnover back to the principal happens once that assessment is complete.

Student & personnel reunification

Account for all students, teachers, and staff. Establish a safe, controlled reunification area and release students only to authorized parents/guardians or designated persons.

Typically the covered court — that's your reunification point.

Medical & psychological support

Provide or facilitate medical attention for injured persons. Arrange psychological and emotional support for students, teachers, staff, and affected families.

Set up an evaluation process for post-traumatic stress — watch especially for anyone who witnessed harm to a classmate, friend, or family member during the incident.

Communication & information management

Provide verified updates to parents, personnel, and the school community through one agreed, official channel. Correct misinformation and discourage unverified reports. Protect the privacy of victims, students, and families.

A PTA meeting can double as a debriefing venue. Route official posts through a small, defined team so updates only ever come from one verified source.

Investigation support

Cooperate fully with law enforcement and other authorized investigating agencies. Preserve relevant records, CCTV footage, communications, and other information as required. Avoid disturbing any area identified as part of the investigation.

CCTV footage is important evidence. A case typically moves: investigation → fiscal → apprehension → prosecution → court. Treat the scene as evidence to be preserved, not cleaned up.

Restoration of school operations

Determine when classes and activities can safely resume. Provide alternative arrangements if portions of the school remain unavailable.

Resume only once everything required is done — especially post-investigation scene processing (SOCO). Reassess access as any police line is lifted.

After-action review

Conduct a formal review of the incident. Identify what worked well and what needs improvement. Review emergency communication, lockdown/lockout procedures, coordination with law enforcement, evacuation/accountability, and reunification — measured against your own localized contingency plan. Update the Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan based on the findings.

For scheduled drills, this review happens quarterly through the school year. On a synchronized drill day, PNP observes and critiques — rating preparation and performance and offering advice — rather than running the drill themselves. The school's own Drill Critic records a separate, official internal critique for the After-Action Report — the outside observer's evaluation (LGU/PNP/BFP/Local DRRM, per the Legal basis section) is independent of it, not a substitute for it.
